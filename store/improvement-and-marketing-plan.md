# Improvement and marketing plan — Dry Eyes Blink Reminder Lite

23 October 2026. Written after reading the BlinkGuard listing saved in
`competitors/`, the Chrome listing saved beside it, the Windows Partner Center
export in `Blink app stats - 21 OCT 2026/`, and the publishing notes already
in this folder.

This file is both plans:

- **Improvements** — what to change in the product and on the store shelf, from the BlinkGuard comparison, and what not to build.
- **Marketing** — the one-page website, the YouTube videos, the clinic card, and the order in which to publish them.

How to make the site is under "The website." How to make the videos is under "YouTube."

This replaces the 21 October line in `PLAN.md` that deferred a website and
YouTube as "high effort, uncertain return." A one-page site and two short
videos are worth doing. A channel, a brand campaign, and a translated content
operation are not. The audience and the sentence stay as already decided:
people whose eye doctor told them to blink fully, and "helps you follow that
advice without interrupting your work." Never claim it treats anything.

## Improvements

BlinkGuard sells one timer, dressed as both a blink habit and the 20-20-20
rule. Its posters offer 10, 15 or 20 minutes, and profiles at 4, 8, 15 and
20. A nudge every ten minutes does not restore a blink. Yours keeps the two
jobs apart: a frequent blink cue (every 60 seconds on the published
extension, every 30 in the unreleased 1.1.0) and a separate look-away on the
clock. That split is the product. The listing should say it in the first
sentence.

Their five store images are illustrated slides, one sentence each, in colours
that survive as a thumbnail. Yours are five photographs of the same dark
article, with BLINK, TAKE A BREAK, CUSTOMISABLE, SNOOZE ANY TIME and WORKING
QUIETLY large enough to hide the feature. The small promo tile, "Blink more
often," is the one asset already in their league. Chrome's own listing
guidance asks for shots of the real experience, consistent with the icon, and
warns that keyword lists can get an item suspended. Catch up on clarity.
Do not replace honest screenshots with posters of features the app does not
have.

Take from them, in the product, only a row of named starting points that set
both timers ("blink fully," "a long day," "only the look-away"). A plain
"reminders today" count is fair. Leave the rest: an "eye health: Good"
score, a screen-time number, and Night Shift. A warm tint is a different
product, it wants a permanent overlay on every page, and it would spend the
optional-permission story to imitate a setting Windows already has.

The permission difference is the other thing to keep and to say out loud.
BlinkGuard's slides say the reminder works on every tab, which means it asks
for every site at install. Yours draws nothing until the person allows it.
That loses a few impatient installs and it is the strongest line on the
listing. The 1.1.0 badge, for "running, but nowhere to draw," is what stops
that wait feeling like a broken install. Ship the badge with the new
screenshots, not before them.

The published extension chimes at 60% volume every minute, because a dot at
35% opacity is easy to miss. Their listing promises silence by default.
When 1.1.0 ships, prefer a word for the first reminders (the tutorial
already says "close, squeeze, open"), a quieter blink, and the sound kept
on the rarer break. The author lives with a chime. A stranger did not ask
for one.

"Lite" should mean "the same reminder, in the browser." The Windows app is
the moat: the cue over every program, not only over a tab. That sentence
belongs near the top of both listings. It is currently the last line of the
Chrome description.

## What not to claim

The useful sentences are already in the listing and the privacy policy.
Screen use is associated with blinking less often, and with blinks that do
not fully close. The app times a cue to blink and a separate cue to look
away. It does not watch the eyes, read the page, or send anything anywhere.

Stop before any sentence a clinician would have to defend:

- It does not treat, cure, reduce, or prevent dry eye, eye strain, or
  blurred vision.
- It is not clinically proven, and a disclaimer does not rescue a treatment
  claim. The US FTC has fined a vision app (Ultimeyes, 2015) for improvement
  claims the studies did not support. The same standard covers apps the FDA
  never reviews.
- User quotes about eyes feeling better are treatment claims if you publish
  them as advertising. Do not solicit them and do not put them on the site.
- "Eye health: Good" is a medical costume. Do not add it.

One disclaimer sentence, on the site and in both detailed descriptions, is
enough: this reminds you to do a habit you may already have been given. It
does not diagnose or treat anything. If your eyes hurt, see an eye-care
professional.

Chrome will also suspend a listing that reads as a keyword list. Use
"dry eyes," "blink," "look away," and "20-20-20" inside sentences. Do not
add a paragraph of synonyms. Microsoft gives you seven search terms of 30
characters; the incomplete-blinking import on the Desktop is the right place
for those, and it should ship with Windows 1.4.0, which is a review you have
already chosen to invite.

## Marketing

Three phases. Phase A needs no store review. Phase B is the next time you
are willing to be reviewed. Phase C only happens if the people from B are
still there a month later.

### Phase A — now, without asking Google to look

1. Publish the one-page site in the section below. Point the Chrome
   dashboard's website field at it only when you next submit, so the field
   change is not its own review. The page can go live before that. The
   privacy-policy URL already filed with both stores stays where it is
   (`store/privacy-policy.md` on GitHub). The new page links to it. Do not
   move it.
2. Cut one 40-second video from the Windows app, by the method below. Upload
   it unlisted. Replace the 6-second Microsoft trailer with a 1920×1080
   export of the same piece when you submit 1.4.0. Microsoft wants the file,
   not a YouTube link, at 1920×1080, H.264, with an audio track even if the
   track is silent. A 16:9 hero image with no text has to sit behind that
   trailer or the Store will not put it at the top. The current hero still
   has English text; that item is already on the October list.
3. Leave the Chrome package at 1.0.0. Draft the five replacement screenshots
   and the first two lines of the detailed description on disk, so the
   submission is a folder rather than a decision made in the dashboard.
4. Finish AlternativeTo. The copy in `promo-submissions.md` is ready. After
   it is approved, suggest the app from EyeLeo's page, then Stretchly,
   Workrave and BreakTimer, one at a time. The sentence that separates it
   from those four is already written: they block the screen, this never
   does. Do not like the listing yourself.
5. Print the clinic card, also below, and give it to your own optometrist
   before anyone else. No bulk email to clinics.
6. Ship Windows 1.4.0 when the build is ready, with the search-term import
   and the new trailer. That is the Microsoft review already planned. It is
   not a Chrome review.

### Phase B — one Chrome submission, when the listing is ready

Upload 1.1.0 together with the new screenshots, the detailed description,
the website URL, and the YouTube URL. One review, not four. The YouTube
video is shown before the screenshots on a Chrome listing, so a bad video
would lead the page. Wait until the 40-second cut is one you would press
play on.

After it is public, and not before:

- One LinkedIn post. The story already sketched in `PLAN.md`: dry eyes, the
  doctor said blink fully, a tool in 30 languages, a rating helps if you
  actually use it. One post. Not a series.
- Add Chrome as a platform on AlternativeTo, with the site as the official
  URL instead of only the Microsoft Store.
- One note in a builder community (`r/chrome_extensions` or `r/SideProject`),
  written as what you made and what you are unsure of. Not in `r/Dryeyes`
  and not in `r/optometry`. Those are patients and clinicians. A launch post
  there is the wrong room, and the mods will remove it.

### Phase C — only if a month of Phase B still has users

- The second video: installing the extension and what "allow on this site"
  means, with the 1.1.0 badge visible. Sixty to ninety seconds.
- Ask people who have really used it for a few days to rate the version they
  use. No discount, no donation tied to a rating, no second accounts. The
  FTC's review rules and both stores' rules already say this. The October
  plan said it too.
- Product Hunt is optional and easy to waste. It fits a launch with a site,
  a video, and a listing you will not be ashamed of in the comments. It does
  not fit a free habit tool with no account: hunters cannot "try a
  workflow," they can only install. If you do it, the line is the cue you
  can click through and the permission you are not asked for. Tuesday to
  Thursday, shortly after midnight Pacific, and then stay for the comments.
  Skip it without regret if that week is busy.
- A second language on the website only if Partner Center or the Chrome
  dashboard shows a country actually arriving. The stores are already
  translated into 30 languages. The site and the videos should not be.

### Do these later, or not at all

| Skip | Why |
|---|---|
| Paid ads | Nothing to pay them back with. A donation app does not buy Google ads, and health-related ad policies are strict. |
| An X account | Already decided. Posts do not last, and the audience is not there. |

| Softpedia, MajorGeeks, Uptodown | Low value, and the exe is unsigned. Do them only after the site exists, and keep pointing at the Store first. The kit is in `promo-submissions.md`. |

| Emailing clinics | One card, handed to someone who already knows you. |
| Posting in dry-eye patient groups | Answer a person who asks how you remember to blink. Do not arrive with a link. |
| 30 translated videos | The listings already carry the languages. |

## The website

### What it is for

One page, so a link from a clinic card, a YouTube description, AlternativeTo,
or a friend's message has somewhere to land that offers Windows and Chrome
and repeats the privacy promise. It is also the URL the Chrome listing is
missing. Google's listing guidance treats a website and a support URL as
trust, and the verified-publisher line on a Chrome listing is a link you
control. That badge is earned by a clean record over months, not by the
page, but the page has to exist before the link can.

It is not a blog, a docs site, or a shop.

### What to put on it

In this order, and almost nothing else:

1. **Blink more often.** Under it: a gentle cue to blink fully, and a
   separate reminder to look away. You can click straight through both.
2. Two buttons. Windows goes to
   `https://apps.microsoft.com/detail/9NBRV5W24KH5`. Chrome waits until
   Phase B, then goes to the public listing. Until then the Chrome button
   can say it is coming, or be absent. Do not send people to a listing you
   are about to replace.
3. The 40-second video.
4. Three short blocks: two timers, click-through, nothing leaves the
   device. The third block links to the privacy policy.
5. The disclaimer sentence.
6. "Also over every other program, on Windows." One line, not a second
   product.
7. Footer: support email `fabtaipei@gmail.com`, GitHub issues, source. The
   donation link goes here, not above the fold. The page is not a tip jar.

No analytics, no Meta pixel, no embedded comment form, no cookie banner
that sets a cookie. The app promises it does not phone home. The page
should be able to say the same thing. YouTube's player is the one
exception, and only if the video is on the page; the player sets YouTube's
cookies, so a plain link to the video is the cleaner choice and a facade
image that clicks through to YouTube is enough. Count visitors in Partner
Center and the Chrome dashboard, which you already have. Those numbers are
the stores measuring their own pages, not you measuring your users.

### How to make it

The repo is already public at `github.com/fabtaipei/blinkreminder`. GitHub
Pages will serve a static site from it for nothing.

Do not turn on Pages for the repository root, and do not point it at the
current `docs/` folder. `docs/` holds design notes. Publishing it would put
those notes on the internet under your product's name.

Add a `site/` directory with a single `index.html`, a copy of the privacy
policy as `privacy.html` that matches `store/privacy-policy.md`, and the
lime-on-black tile you already have (`store/chrome/promo-440x280.png` and
the 128px icon). Write the page by hand. No framework, no build, no webfont
hosted by a third party. System fonts, the lime you already use (`#bde401`)
on near-black, the page readable if the stylesheet fails.

Publish with a GitHub Action that uploads only `site/` as a Pages artifact.
The official starter is `actions/upload-pages-artifact` followed by
`actions/deploy-pages`, on a push to `main` that touches `site/`. The site
then lives at `https://fabtaipei.github.io/blinkreminder/`. That URL is good
enough to put on the Chrome listing and on the clinic card.

Buy a domain only after the page has been up for a week and you still like
it. A `.com` you can say out loud is worth about the price of a coffee per
year, because a clinic card and a verified-publisher link both want a name
that is not a GitHub path. Do not block the page on the domain. Candidates
worth checking before anyone else registers them: something with "blink" and
your existing name, not "dryeye-cure" and not a medical word you would have
to defend. Point the domain at the Pages site with the two DNS records
GitHub shows you. HTTPS is automatic.

Keep the Chrome and Microsoft privacy-policy URLs on the GitHub blob until
you deliberately change them in a submission. A 404 on a filed privacy URL
is a review problem. The new `privacy.html` can be the nicer copy; both
must say the same things.

### A page you can finish in an evening

Open `site/index.html` in a browser, tab through it with a keyboard, and
read it on a phone width. The test is whether a person who has never seen
the app can say, after twenty seconds, what the two reminders are and where
to install the one they need. If the page needs a second screen of
explanation, cut.

## YouTube

### What the videos are for

Chrome shows a YouTube link ahead of the screenshots. Microsoft shows an
uploaded trailer at the top of the listing once a text-free 16:9 hero sits
behind it. The same recording feeds both, plus the website. That is the
whole return. Search traffic from YouTube to a 40-second silent demo will
be small, and that is fine.

You already have the right raw idea in `store/trailer/`. The file is 6.3
seconds: a real desktop, one blink dot, one break dim, captions that start
with "Caption:" so nobody thinks the app printed them, a silent audio track
because Microsoft's checker rejects a video with no audio. It is too short
to explain anything, and too short for a YouTube end screen. It is the right
discipline. Extend that discipline. Do not stage a fake interface.

### The three videos, and only these

**1. The cue. About 40 seconds.** Make this first, from the Windows app, so
it can also replace the Store trailer.

| Second | Picture | Words on screen |
|---|---|---|
| 0–4 | A real desktop, a document, nothing else | Blink more often. |
| 4–14 | The blink cue, the real default dot, then the word | A cue to blink. Click straight through it. |
| 14–24 | The break, the real dim | And, on the clock, a cue to look away. |
| 24–34 | Typing continues through a second blink | It never takes focus. |
| 34–40 | Lime dot on black, the name, both stores named in text | Dry Eyes Blink Reminder Lite. Windows and Chrome. |

No music you do not own. Silence with burned-in captions is better than a
library track. No face, no voiceover required. If you do speak, say only
the lines in the table. Do not say the eyes will feel better.

**2. Allowing the page. 60–90 seconds.** Make this in Phase C, after 1.1.0
is the published extension, so the video matches the install.

Show: the Chrome Web Store install, the toolbar with the "nowhere to draw"
state, the welcome page, the permission prompt, then the cue appearing on
the page you were reading. Say, in captions: until you allow it, blink
reminders are not drawn on the page, and break reminders still arrive as
notifications. Nothing on the page is read.

**3. Two different habits. Optional, later, only if you want a third.**
Sixty seconds. "Eye doctors often suggest two habits: blink fully, and look
away on a schedule. They are not the same timer." Then show the two cards
in settings. Stop. This is the video that becomes a treatment claim if the
script wanders. Write the captions down first and do not improvise.

Do not make a Shorts strategy. If video 1 is done, a vertical crop of the
middle twenty seconds can be uploaded as a Short with the same title. One
Short. The discovery there is a lottery and not part of the plan.

### How to record

Use the Windows app, on the machine where you already know the cue looks
right. Xbox Game Bar (Win+Alt+R) records a window or the screen at 1080p
and is already installed. OBS is the alternative if Game Bar drops frames.

Before you press record, do what `store/trailer/SCRIPT.md` already requires:

- The capture screen has no other windows, no notifications, no personal
  documents. A dummy page or a blank document is the picture.
- Wait until you are not inside a minute of the wall-clock break, or the
  app will fire its own reminder in the middle of the take.
- Set colour, strength and size to the defaults a new user gets, not to a
  tuned setup. The video is an advertisement for the first run.
- Record 1920×1080, 30 fps. Leave two seconds of stillness at each end so
  a cut does not clip a fade.

Edit in Clipchamp, which is on Windows 11, or extend the trailer script you
already trust if you would rather the captions were placed by code.
Captions are burned into the picture, not only uploaded as a YouTube
subtitle file, because the Microsoft trailer and a muted autoplay both need
them visible. Keep the "Caption:" prefix the trailer notes invented, for
any line the app itself did not draw. The app's own word ("BLINK") needs
no prefix.

Export H.264, 1920×1080, with a stereo AAC track. Microsoft's checker
rejects a file with no audio stream; a silent track is the accepted fix and
you already use it. Keep the file under a few dozen megabytes. The Store
limit is far higher (2 GB). Thumbnail: the lime dot on black, three words,
the same idea as `store/chrome/promo-440x280.png`. YouTube's custom
thumbnail wants 1280×720.

### How to upload

One channel is enough. The Google account that owns the Chrome listing, or
a channel named KennyTechy, not a fresh brand you then have to feed. Channel
art can be the marquee tile. The About box is four lines: what the app does,
the disclaimer, the website, the support email.

Upload video 1 as **Unlisted**. Watch it on a phone with the sound off. If
the cue is invisible, the take is wrong; do not "fix" a faint default by
raising the opacity only for the video. Either the default is strong enough
to see on video, or video 1 should show the word style and say so.

When it is good enough, set it to **Public**. Title:

> Blink reminder: a cue you can click through

Description, in this order: one sentence on the two reminders, the
disclaimer, the website, the Microsoft Store link, the Chrome link once it
exists, the GitHub link. A few real tags (`blink reminder`, `dry eyes`,
`20-20-20`, `eye strain`). No tag list of every synonym you can think of.

Paste that YouTube URL into the Chrome listing's promotional-video field in
Phase B. For Microsoft, upload the mp4 and a 1920×1080 PNG thumbnail in
Partner Center with 1.4.0, and clear the text off the 16:9 hero so the
trailer is allowed at the top. Captions on the Store copy should also be
uploaded as a WebVTT file; Partner Center accepts one and it is the
accessible version of the burned-in text.

There is no end screen worth adding under 40 seconds, and YouTube only
shows end screens in the last 5–20 seconds of longer videos. A spoken "link
in the description" is unnecessary if the last frame names the app.

## The clinic card

This is the channel that matches the audience, and it was already on the
October list. One half-page, not a brochure.

The front is a sentence a clinician can hand over without endorsing a
treatment: "A timer for two habits: blink fully, and look away from the
screen. It does not watch your eyes." A QR code to the website. The name.
The back, in smaller type, is Windows and Chrome, the disclaimer, and your
email.

Make it in the browser: one HTML page at A5, print to PDF. No clinical
logo, no "recommended by," no before-and-after. Give the first ten to the
practice you already attend. If they will not put it out, the channel is
closed and you have lost an afternoon, not a quarter.

## What to say, everywhere

Use the lines you already trust. They are in `promo-submissions.md` and on
the Chrome listing.

> A gentle cue to blink, and a separate one to look away. You can click
> straight through both.

> Free, no ads, no account, nothing collected. The code is public.

> On Windows it sits over every program. In Chrome and Edge it sits over
> the page, and only after you allow it.

Vary the order to fit the box. Do not vary the facts. When a sentence grows
a promise about symptoms, delete it.

## How you will know

Look once a month, not every day.

| | Now (October 2026) | In three months | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| Windows installs in the month | 41 | 150, if the site and the trailer are up | Partner Center, Acquisitions |
| Windows page views | 247 | worth more than the install count | same |
| Views that install | 16.6% | hold this; investigate if it falls under 10% | same |
| Machines still running it | 11 | 40 | Partner Center, monthly active devices |
| Windows ratings | 2 | 15, from people who use it | Reviews |
| Chrome installs | new, unrated | whatever the first month is; write it down | Chrome dashboard |
| Chrome listing has website, video, five real screenshots | no | yes, in the one Phase B submission | the listing |
| Supporters | 2 | 10 | the donation page |

The October plan's "300 installs" assumed a steeper curve than a clinic
card and a better listing will produce. One hundred and fifty Windows
installs in a month, with the conversion held, is a doubling of arrival
and a success. If views do not move and conversion stays at 16%, the
listings are fine and the links are not getting sent. If views move and
conversion falls, the new trailer or the new first sentence is the suspect,
not "marketing" in general.

Chrome has no baseline yet. The first month after Phase B is the baseline.
Do not compare it with BlinkGuard's 11 users and declare a winner.
