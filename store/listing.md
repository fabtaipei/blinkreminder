# Store listing copy

> **The copy below is the reasoning, not the text to paste.** It still carries
> markdown and the old download-size line. The text that actually goes into
> Partner Center lives in `listing-paste.txt`, in plain text, fenced field by
> field — pasting from this file is how the Description box ended up holding
> the short description and a stray `---`.

Every sentence here describes what the **software does**, never what the
user's eyes will become. That rule is not squeamishness: Store Policy 10.10.1
requires advertising to comply with applicable law, which pulls FTC standards
into a certification decision, and the FTC has fined an app developer for
unsubstantiated vision claims.

**Never use, anywhere in the listing or in screenshots:**

- "clinically proven", "science-backed", "evidence-based" — these are FTC
  *establishment claims* and require randomised controlled human trials **of
  this product**
- the name of any condition: dry eye, computer vision syndrome, digital eye
  strain, asthenopia
- FDA's verbs: prevent, treat, relieve, cure, mitigate, diagnose, restore
- links to studies (PubMed and the rest) — citing third-party literature in
  advertising makes that study *your* claim
- a "not a medical device" disclaimer as cover for bolder copy; the FTC treats
  that as contradictory and ineffective
- stock imagery of someone rubbing their eyes

**Category:** Utilities + tools. **Not Medical** — that category is for health
records, so it would be inaccurate under 10.1.1 and invites a line-by-line
search for device claims.

**Price:** Free.

## Description

> Dry Eyes Blink Reminder Lite is a small, quiet utility that lives in your system tray and
> gives you a brief visual cue to blink — and, on a separate timer, to look
> away from the screen for a moment.
>
> The cue is deliberately subtle. It is a soft pulse, a dot, or a single word,
> shown for a fraction of a second and then gone. You can click straight
> through it: it never takes focus, never steals a keystroke, and never gets in
> the way of what you were doing.
>
> **Two independent reminders**
> The blink reminder fires on an interval you choose — anything from a few
> seconds upward. The look-away reminder runs on the wall clock, so it lands at
> the same minutes past the hour no matter when you started the app. Each has
> its own completely separate settings: its own style, colour, size, position,
> timing and sound. Changing one never touches the other.
>
> **Made for more than one screen**
> Reminders appear on every connected monitor, correctly sized on each one even
> when your displays run at different scaling. A small indicator dot in the
> corner of each screen shows the app is running, with a countdown to the next
> break above it.
>
> **Yours to tune**
> Choose a gentle dim, a coloured dot, or a word of your own. Set the size,
> position, opacity, fade and hold. Pick from four sounds, each with its own
> volume, or leave it silent. Preview any change before you save it.
>
> **Small and quiet**
> A single download of around 20 MB that uses roughly 25 MB of memory while
> running. No account, no sign-in, no advertising, no analytics, and no
> network connection of any kind — the app never contacts any server,
> including ours. It does not use your camera.
>
> **Notes**
> Dry Eyes Blink Reminder Lite runs in the notification area and has no main window.
> Right-click its tray icon for Settings. The reminder pulse is rate-limited so
> that no setting can exceed three flashes per second.

## Product features (up to 20, each ≤200 characters)

1. A brief, gentle cue to blink — on a timer you choose, from a few seconds upward
2. A separate look-away reminder on the wall clock, with its own independent settings
3. Reminders on every monitor, correctly sized even across different display scaling
4. Click-through overlays that never take focus or interrupt your typing
5. Choose a soft dim, a coloured dot, or a word of your own
6. Four sounds, each with its own volume — or silence
7. Preview any change before you save it
8. Around 20 MB to download, roughly 25 MB of memory while running
9. No account, no advertising, no analytics, no network connection
10. No camera access — it works on a timer, it does not watch you
